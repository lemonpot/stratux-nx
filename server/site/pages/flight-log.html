Block time and air time, logged automatically

What block time and air time mean, which one goes in your logbook, and how Stratux NX records both from GPS on every flight, without you pressing anything.

The four times of a flight

Airlines describe each flight with four times, often called OOOI:

TimeWhenAlso called
OutThe aircraft starts moving from its parking spotOff-block, block out
OffThe wheels leave the runwayTakeoff, airborne
OnThe wheels touch the runwayLanding, touchdown
InThe aircraft stops at the end of the flightOn-block, block in
  • Block time runs from Out to In. It includes the taxi.
  • Air time runs from Off to On, wheels up to wheels down.
  • The difference is taxi out (Out to Off) plus taxi in (On to In).

Which one goes in your logbook

For airplanes, the regulators define flight time in a way that matches block time:

  • FAA: 14 CFR 1.1 defines flight time as pilot time that starts when the aircraft moves under its own power for the purpose of flight and ends when it comes to rest after landing.
  • EASA: FCL.010 defines flight time for aeroplanes as the time from when the aircraft first moves for the purpose of taking off until it finally comes to rest at the end of the flight.

Air time is closer to what 14 CFR 1.1 calls time in service for maintenance records: from leaving the ground to touching down. Some operators and forms ask for both, which is why it helps to have both.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Check what your authority, school or operator asks you to log.

How Stratux NX detects each time

The Stratux turns on with the aircraft's power, so it is already running when you start moving. It reads the GPS every second and decides:

  • Off-block: the aircraft moves above taxi speed for a few seconds. Short GPS jumps on the ground are ignored.
  • Takeoff and landing: from sustained speed and climb or descent. Touch-and-goes are counted.
  • On-block: the aircraft stays stopped (2 knots or less) for 45 seconds after landing. The flight is then closed.
  • Airports: the nearest airport at departure and arrival, from an offline database of 84,512 airports.
  • Time zones: all times are stored in UTC/Zulu and shown in local time, which follows the aircraft's position.

What is saved with each flight

  • The four times, block time, air time, taxi out and taxi in.
  • The route, with position, altitude, groundspeed and vertical speed every second.
  • Distance, maximum groundspeed and maximum GPS altitude.
  • The FIS-B weather received during the flight (METARs, TAFs and NEXRAD), and the traffic around you.

Flights are written to the SD card while they happen. If the power is cut, the flight is recovered on the next boot. If the cut happened in the air, the flight is kept and marked as incomplete.

Accuracy

  • All times come from GPS and are accurate to a few seconds.
  • Off-block is when the aircraft starts moving, not when the engine starts. Hobbs and tach times measure different things and will not match.
  • Altitude is GPS altitude, which can differ from indicated or pressure altitude.
  • Review the times before copying them to your logbook. You remain responsible for your logbook entries.
