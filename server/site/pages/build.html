How to build a Stratux

A Stratux is a Raspberry Pi with one or two small radios, a GPS and a case. A standard build needs no soldering and takes an afternoon. This page lists the parts, which radios you need where you fly, and how to put it together.

Parts list

PartWhat it doesNotes
Raspberry PiThe computer that runs the softwareThe 3B and 3B+ are the usual choice. A Pi 4 works but runs hotter and uses more power.
microSD cardHolds the software and your flights16 GB or larger, from a known brand.
1090 MHz radioReceives 1090ES ADS-B trafficAn RTL-SDR USB radio. Useful everywhere.
978 MHz radioReceives UAT traffic and FIS-B weatherUnited States only.
868 MHz radio (optional)Receives OGN / FLARM-compatible trafficMainly useful in Europe, where gliders and light aircraft use it.
AntennasOne per radioEach antenna must match its radio's frequency.
GPSYour position, and accurate time for the flight logA USB GPS with WAAS/SBAS. Recent u-blox chips (8, 9 or 10) work best.
Case and fanProtects the Pi and keeps it coolKeep the vents clear in a hot cockpit.
AHRS board (optional)Attitude for synthetic vision in some appsA backup aid only, not for IFR.
PowerA USB battery pack or a cockpit USB supplyAt least 2.5 A, 3 A for a Pi 4. Weak supplies cause reboots.

Which radios you need

Where you flyRadiosWhat you get
United States1090 + 978 MHzAll ADS-B traffic and FIS-B weather (METARs, TAFs, NEXRAD, TFRs, and more)
Europe1090 + 868 MHzADS-B traffic plus OGN / FLARM-compatible traffic
Elsewhere1090 MHzADS-B traffic from equipped aircraft

Near the US border you may also receive 978 MHz traffic and weather from US ground stations. A Stratux only receives. It does not make your aircraft visible to others.

Putting it together

  1. Write the software to the microSD card. See download and install.
  2. Fit the Raspberry Pi in the bottom of the case, with the fan if your case has one.
  3. Plug in the radios and GPS to the USB ports. Label the radios so you know which antenna goes on which.
  4. Screw on the antennas by hand. Pliers can break the small connectors.
  5. Insert the microSD card, close the case and connect power.
  6. Join the Stratux Wi-Fi and open http://192.168.10.1. The status page shows each radio and the GPS.
Give the GPS a clear view of the sky the first time. The first fix can take 10 to 20 minutes with a new GPS.

Where to put it in the aircraft

  • Antennas work best vertical and away from metal, headsets and other electronics.
  • The GPS needs a view of the sky, so near the windshield or a side window usually works.
  • Secure it so it can't move into the controls, and keep it out of direct sun if you can.

Buying one ready-made

If you'd rather not build one, several vendors sell complete Stratux receivers. In late 2026 a prebuilt dual-band Stratux sells for around $440. Building your own usually costs less, depending on the parts. Any standard Stratux can run Stratux NX: flash a new card and keep the original as a backup.

The software is free either way

Stratux and Stratux NX are both free and open source. Stratux NX adds an automatic flight log, data limits for Starlink, online updates and a redesigned interface, on the same hardware. See how they compare.

For more build options (other radios, serial GPS, OGN transmitters, displays), see the upstream Stratux project and its wiki.
